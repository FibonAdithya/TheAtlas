Many times across an adventure, brave adventurers must venture into a dungeon. While classically underground crypts, lairs and caves the dungeon design can extend to any area where there are distinct subareas (rooms, caverns, districts) and antagonists who the group may face.

First we come up with the themes and aspects of a dungeon. These are important characteristics that we are using to set the tone and denizens.

Then we make a list of possible rooms, chambers/areas. When creating a dungeon think about:
- How do they typically get in?
- What do they eat/drink (do they need a place to grow, cook, store their food)
- How do they deal with enviromental issues (stay warm, cool, dry etc.)
- Where do they sleep, work, relax etc.
- Plot areas (ritual chambers, treasure rooms, trap rooms)
Then imagine all the different ways that they might interact with each other. Usability of the space is important because many creatures design their lives to minimise friction (e.g. kitchen near storeroom).

This process should give various paths through the dungeon that a denizen might take. E.g.
Farm -> Animal pens -> storeroom -> kitchen -> messhall -> bedrooms.
Consider how these link together should give a graph with many loops. 
Expand: Why loops are good game design

Next we write a quick description of each room, this is to give us a quick idea. But I would recommend not populating it with encounters yet. 

Instead we write [[Encounter Tables]] to create a dynamic dungeon enviroment where it's denizens are active participants rather than passive statues waiting for players to kick down the door.
